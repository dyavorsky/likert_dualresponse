Dan's draft:

Dual-response conjoint asks a binary follow-up question: "would you actually buy it?" We provide the behavioral model and likelihood for its natural extension to an ordinal follow-up: "how likely are you to buy it?" We compare our approach to two common shortcuts (collapsing the scale to yes/no via top-box, and assigning each scale point a fixed purchase probability) and show that both can bias profit expectations. Geoff's edit:

Dual-response conjoint first asks consumers to choose their most preferred product among the displayed profiles and only then asks the respondent whether she would purchase her selection. We provide a behavioral model that rationalizes this frequently used design and derive the closed form likelihood. By modeling the latter decision as a probability, rather than a binary decision, we nest several common survey designs: a binary followup; a yes/no binary; as well as an ordinal scale.


Final version:

Dual-response conjoint first asks respondents to choose their most preferred product among the displayed profiles and then asks the respondent whether she would purchase her selection. We provide a behavioral model that rationalizes this frequently used design and derive the closed form likelihood. By modeling the latter decision as a probability rather than a binary choice, we nest the current binary design and show the behavioral assumption implied by it. We also assess the effects of two common shortcuts: dichotomizing the ordinal scale to yes/no via top-box, and assigning each scale point a fixed purchase probability.
